# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Ambitious high-school students and the counselors, coaches, or mentors helping them interpret extracurricular development. They use the desktop app to compare a student's real activities with credible, synthetic archetypes without needing to understand the underlying research dataset or admissions jargon.

## Product Purpose

Turn the project's public-evidence research into an explorable desktop atlas of highly developed student profiles. The first version presents eight synthetic, de-identified model students: Olympiad, Research, Robotics, Debate/Quizbowl, Athlete/Well-rounded, Humanities, Arts, and Builder/Entrepreneurship.

Users can enter activities, add quality-defining context, and receive a profile showing their closest archetypes, demonstrated strengths, underdeveloped dimensions, evidence confidence, and useful next steps. Achievement tiers describe the strength and rarity of a documented signal, not admission probability. The product must not claim that any activity guarantees admission or calculate unsupported admissions odds.

Success means users leave with a more accurate vocabulary for evaluating depth, distinction, initiative, and external validation; understand why superficially similar activities can differ in quality; and can identify a few constructive next steps without being pushed into prestige-chasing.

## Brand Personality

Precise, calm, and evidence-first. The product should feel like a trustworthy field atlas with a supportive expert nearby: clear enough for a student, rigorous enough for a counselor, and candid about uncertainty.

## Anti-references

- Dense spreadsheet or database interfaces that expose the research schema instead of answering the user's question.
- Gamified admissions-chance calculators, acceptance meters, or score theatrics that imply unsupported precision.
- Luxury admissions-consulting aesthetics that manufacture authority through exclusivity.
- Competitive leaderboards or social comparison mechanics that turn development into status ranking.
- Generic productivity dashboards made from interchangeable cards and decorative metrics.

## Design Principles

1. **Explain every judgment.** A tier or match must reveal the evidence, modifiers, and uncertainty behind it.
2. **Compare patterns, not people.** Use synthetic composites and developmental dimensions rather than named-student imitation.
3. **Context changes quality.** Evaluate ownership, selectivity, output, validation, duration, and impact instead of relying on activity labels alone.
4. **Guide without predicting.** Show strengths, gaps, and next steps while refusing admissions guarantees or invented odds.
5. **Progressive depth.** Make the first comparison simple, then let users inspect rubrics, examples, sources, and caveats when useful.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

A dedicated accessibility audit is deferred until after the first functional version. The foundation will still use semantic controls, visible focus states, keyboard-operable workflows, readable contrast, color-independent tier labels, and reduced-motion handling so accessibility is not treated as a retrofit. Copy must avoid shaming students with fewer resources and distinguish access-dependent opportunities from demonstrated skill.
